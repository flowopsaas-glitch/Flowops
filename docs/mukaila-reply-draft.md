Thanks for coming back to me, Mukaila. Happy to answer all three — but I still need

**What we sell, and to whom**

FlowOps is freight and logistics operating software for South African and African
operators. Three things we do that they care about:

1. **Cross-border forwarding** — landed cost, HS codes, duty and VAT, general average.
2. **Rail & Intermodal corridor coordination** — planning and tracking loads across
   rail corridors. This is our highest-value capability and it is the main reason a
   large operator buys our top tier.
3. **Courier and last-mile management** — fleet, drivers, waybills, POD, dispatch.

Who buys it — and this is the part most people get wrong about our market:

- **Courier and last-mile companies.** The ordinary ones. Think JKJ Courier, EPX.
  A courier running 10 to 50 vehicles. These are our bread and butter and they are
  the *easiest* conversations, because the pain is obvious and the buyer is usually
  the owner.
- **Freight forwarders, clearing agents and 3PLs.** Cross-border and multi-modal
  operators, typically moving containers or groupage.

Both. Not just the second group. If you only go after forwarders you are fishing in
half the pond.

**What a client is worth (first-year contract value)**

| Customer | Monthly | First year |
|---|---|---|
| Courier / mid-market operator (10 drivers) | R9,999 | **R120,000** |
| Courier / mid-market operator (25 drivers) | R19,999 | **R240,000** |
| Large operator, unlimited (Enterprise) | R250,000 | **R3,000,000** |

So: most deals you bring will land between **R120k and R240k**. The Enterprise tier
is rare and worth roughly **R3m in year one** — that is the one worth real effort,
and it is where I would want you spending most of your time.

**How many I can take**

Realistically **2 to 3 new clients a month**. That is my onboarding and support
capacity, not a target I want exceeded. Quality over volume — I would rather you
brought me two that close than ten that go quiet.

Answer my four and tell me what you think of that structure, and we can move quickly.

the thing to decide before you agree a rate. He is paid a percentage, so when the
Enterprise price went from R100k to R250k/month, every Enterprise deal he closes got

| Rate | Per Enterprise deal (R3.0m) | Per mid-market deal (R240k) |
|---|---|---|
| 5% | R150,000 | R12,000 |
| 10% | **R300,000** | R24,000 |
| 15% | **R450,000** | R36,000 |

A flat 15% is defensible on a R120k–R240k deal (R18k–R36k). It is not defensible on a
R3m deal — R450k for one introduction. **Suggest a tiered rate: 15% on deals up to
R250k, 10% on R250k–R1m, 5% above R1m, or a per-deal cap.** At 2–3 clients/month with
one Enterprise in the mix, a flat 15% is roughly R500k/month in commission.

**Why "first-year value" and "paid on cash collected" matter.** A percentage of
*recurring revenue forever* on an Enterprise client is R250k × rate, every month, for
as long as they stay. That is a permanent liability that grows with your book. "Paid
on cash collected" also stops him being paid for a signature that never becomes money.

**The clawback and the exclusions are not aggressive, they are standard.** Without
the 90-day clawback there is an incentive to bring anyone who will sign. Without
excluding existing pipeline you pay for deals you already had.
